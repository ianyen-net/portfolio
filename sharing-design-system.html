ShareKit: A Sharing Design System Built for Growth

I designed ShareKit, a sharing system that layers a consistent preview and action model on top of the native ShareSheet, and used it to fix fragmented sharing journeys across NotebookLM, Gemini, and Google Maps.

OVERVIEW

The Problem

Sharing Was Inconsistent Across Google

Sharing is how Google apps grow. A good share brings a new person into the product, because the receiver opens something valuable that a sender chose to send. But every first-party app that wanted more than the default system sheet had built its own bespoke sharing UI. The result was fragmentation: different entry points, different previews, different actions, and a different mental model in every app.

I was asked to design a system that gives every first-party app a consistent, data-driven sharing design, without asking people to relearn a new pattern in each place they share from.

The audit covered YouTube, Maps, Gemini, and Photos. The launch partners were NotebookLM, Gemini, and Google Maps.

WHAT THE RESEARCH SHOWED

The Sharing Journey

Was Broken

Defining the sharing journey helped us identify areas of impact. We framed our problems and design principles around these four stages of journey: Discover, Share, Receive, and Re-engage. Across those stages, the audit surfaced the same problems again and again, in plain language:

01
DISCOVER

Inconsistent entry points

Sharing started from different places, with different affordances, depending on the app.

02
SHARE

No clear success confirmation

After tapping a person, a third-party app, or Copy link, users often could not tell whether the share had actually sent.

03
RECEIVE

Anxiety about ambiguous links

Senders and receivers could not see exactly what was being shared when all they got was a link, so trust dropped at the moment of sending and receiving.

04
RE-ENGAGE

Post-share "dead ends"

After a share, there was often nowhere useful to go next, for the sender or the receiver.

Those four problems became the brief for the system. ShareKit did not need to be a new sharing universe. It needed to fix confirmation, entry, preview, and what happens after.

A MODULAR TURN-KEY APPROACH

ShareKit

And Its Design Principles

ShareKit is a suite of offerings that includes a UX interaction framework, front-end library, and back-end infrastructure across mobile and web. Specifically, the UX framework for mobile is called "Splitti", because it visually splits the interface in half. Instead of replacing the system ShareSheet, it layers a preview and action row on top of it.

That layering is the key architectural decision. The system sheet still handles distribution, ranking, and destinations. Splitti owns what the system sheet does not: a consistent preview, clear attribution, and a small set of actions that mean the same thing in every app.

It was built tokens first, then components, with accessibility built in, following the design principles below:

Clear entry points

Elevate clear CTAs in context to shareable content and avoid hiding behind an overflow menu.

Big previews

Provide previews that accurately reflect what is being sent and what the recipient will see in their messaging app.

PROFILE ATTRIBUTION & IDENTITY

Control

Provide tools that enable self-expression and customization of what is being shared to give users more control.

Display metadata

Display accurate metadata for in-chat previews to increase understanding of what has been shared with you.

PREVIEW, CONTENT DESCRIPTION & ACTION

Personal not universal

The final list of app targets and actions to include in the ShareSheet should be based on individual user's usage and context, rather than universal defaults.

ADOPTION

First Party Apps That Prove

Turnkey Works

NotebookLM

NotebookLM shipped artifact sharing on ShareKit on the turnkey approach, with little back-and-forth and no customization required.

This was a 0 to 1 feature. Before ShareKit, NotebookLM had no sharing for artifact sharing at all. Users could generate audio, video, and other outputs inside a notebook, but they could not share any of them with anyone. The output stayed stranded in the notebook. This implementation enabled that experience for the first time.

Gemini

With Gemini, we went beyond implementing Splitti and helped clean up the action bar below each response. Outbound actions such as "Export to Docs" and "Draft in Gmail" were previously scattered across the three-dot menu, which broke from our design principles. Consolidating them in Splitti simplified the bar and matched how users already think about sharing: one place for everything that sends content out.

GEMINI PROMPT SHARING

But Turnkey Doesn't Always Work

App Needed An Extended Tier

After Gemini launched its turnkey ShareKit integration, the team wanted to ship a viral feature ahead of the World Cup: sharing a Gemini-generated image along with the prompt that created it so friends could remix their own version.

Splitti was built for media-first sharing, prioritizing a large visual preview over editable text. Prompt sharing introduced two requirements turnkey couldn't cover: choosing whether to include the prompt, and editing it before sending for privacy. That made it a real capability gap, not a style preference.

Testing the cheaper paths before granting a custom build

Gemini's PM pushed for a custom component from the start, putting us in a familiar design-system dilemma: say no and risk a fork, or grant an ad-hoc waiver and weaken the system. Rather than debating in the room, I prototyped and tested all three paths, starting with turnkey and configured options to get real user evidence.

OPTION 1 · TURNKEY EXPLORATION

PROMPT EDITING

Squeezing the preview, prompt toggle, edit control, and share targets into one turnkey sheet worked against our core principle of preview before action. The toggle pills read like action buttons rather than binary states, leaving users unsure what would actually be sent.

Adding tabs above the preview worked functionally, but failed visually. The tabs and edit controls competed with the image preview, violating one screen, one job and making a quick share feel like an editor.

The third path split the experience into two clear jobs: a pre-share half sheet to toggle and edit the prompt, followed by the standard ShareKit handoff sheet. Users strongly preferred it, giving us clear evidence that a structured new tier was warranted.

EXTENDED HALF SHEET WALKTHROUGH · PROTOTYPE I BUILT

Bend criteria: a governed Extended tier, not an exception

I defined four bend criteria to evaluate when ShareKit should flex:

  • Real gap vs. preference: Can Splitti's existing layout flex, or is a core capability genuinely missing?
  • Pattern vs. one-off feature: Does it solve a recurring user problem beyond a single product?
  • Who else needs it? Reviewing or editing text before sharing also shows up in NotebookLM and AI Mode in Search, pointing to an emerging pattern even with one consumer today.
  • Principle alignment: Does it reinforce ShareKit's principles of clarity, control, and experiential previews?

Turnkey

Adopt Splitti as-is for media-first sharing

Configured

Approved variants within the existing layout model

Extended (Incubated)

Co-built local extension on ShareKit's infrastructure, promoted only with multi-team proof

Incubation & governance registry

Promoting a pattern into an official component carries ongoing maintenance, accessibility, and cross-platform costs, so new patterns have to earn it. Under our incubation model, Gemini's half sheet stays a local extension co-reviewed with our team on ShareKit's backend. I worked with PM and PgM to create a Governance Registry tracking scope, accessibility checks, similar asks, and a fixed review date to decide whether to promote or retire it.

Outcome & a single unified system

Gemini eventually shifted priorities and did not ship prompt sharing due to technical and privacy constraints: privacy required editable prompts, yet sender edits and recipient context both changed the generated remix unless prompts were locked to fixed templates. Even without that launch, the process validated a real capability gap, ruled out cheaper paths with users, and kept a flagship client inside the system.

More broadly, ShareKit unified four distinct Google products, each with its own design language, into one cohesive sharing framework that lifted share completion and is still maintained today.

Learnings

Design for a moving target

In the AI era, products shift while you systematize them. Gemini reprioritized before this feature launched. Measure a system's success by cross-product adoption and research evidence, not a single partner launch.

Bend only on evidence

The Extended tier worked because user research proved turnkey wasn't enough, not because a partner asked loudly. Define your bend criteria upfront so compromise becomes a system principle instead of a one-off precedent.
